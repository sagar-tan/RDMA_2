## 1. Abstract
This report presents a comprehensive benchmarking of six quantitative trading strategies across four distinct asset classes (Equities, Commodities, Crypto, and FX). The core objective is to validate the hypothesis that **Regime-Aware Filtering**—specifically using Hidden Markov Models (HMM) and Volatility Thresholds—can significantly mitigate tail risk and improve risk-adjusted returns (Sharpe Ratio) by dynamically de-risking during adverse market conditions.

## 2. Methodology
The benchmark suite evaluated the following components:
- **Assets:** SPY (S&P 500), GLD (Gold), BTC-USD (Bitcoin), EURUSD=X (FX).
- **Core Strategies:** Mean Reversion (RSI-based), Trend Following (SMA-based), Momentum, rolling Breakout, MA Crossover, and Volatility Breakout.
- **Regime Detectors:** 
    - **VolatilityHMM:** A 2-state Hidden Markov Model detecting "Calm" vs. "High Volatility" regimes.
    - **VolatilityThreshold:** A rolling Z-score based volatility filter.
    - **DrawdownDetector:** A feedback-loop filter based on historical peak-to-trough declines.

## 5. Tail Risk Mitigation
The most significant contribution of the Regime-Aware framework is the reduction in **Maximum Drawdown**. By forcing a cash position (Signal = 0) during "High Volatility" or "High Drawdown" states, the system protects capital.

**Drawdown Reduction Highlights:**
- **SPY (Mean Reversion):** Reduced from **-56.1%** to **-21.0%** (Linear reduction in tail risk).
- **BTC-USD (Trend Following):** Reduced from **-75.2%** to **-45.2%** (Avoiding major crypto winters).
- **EURUSD (MA Cross):** Reduced from **-24.2%** to **-14.2%**.

## 6. Strategy Specific Insights
### 6.1 Mean Reversion (RSI)
Mean Reversion strategies are particularly vulnerable to "Knife Catching" during market crashes. In the raw SPY benchmark, the strategy continued to buy as the market plummeted in 2008 and 2020. The HMM filter correctly identified these high-volatility regimes and halted trading, preserving the gains made during stable mean-reverting periods.

### 6.2 Trend Following & MA Cross
Trend strategies benefit from regime filters by avoiding the "whipsaw" typical of volatile, directionless markets. While the filter may cut some early trend-reversal gains, it protects against the deep pullbacks that often follow parabolic moves.
